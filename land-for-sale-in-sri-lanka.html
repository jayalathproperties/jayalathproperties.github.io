1. What type of land are you looking for?

Start with your purpose. Different land suits different goals.

Residential land

If you plan to build a home, residential land is the right starting point. Popular areas buyers explore:

  • Colombo – close to business, services and facilities.
  • Gampaha – many residential areas with road links to Colombo.
  • Kandy – residential options in the central region.
  • Kurunegala – town and suburban locations.
  • Galle – residential and coastal-region options in the south.
  • Nuwara Eliya – scenic hill-country properties.

Agricultural land

Sri Lanka has different farming regions for different crops. You can look at:

  • Coconut land in Kurunegala and other suitable areas.
  • Tea land in Kandy, Nuwara Eliya and other tea-growing areas.
  • Rubber land in suitable wet-zone areas.
  • Paddy land in farming areas.
  • Cinnamon and spice land.
  • Mixed-crop and farm land.

Before buying farmland, look at soil, water, what is already planted, the access road and labour. Agricultural land does not automatically have permission for houses or commercial use, so check the land-use rules first.

Coconut land

Coconut land suits buyers who want to farm. Ask about the number and condition of the trees, the age of the plantation, past harvest records, water, transport access, upkeep costs and ownership papers. Do not rely on income figures in an advertisement. Ask to see actual production records.

Tea land

For tea land, look at the condition of the bushes, elevation, rainfall, distance to a tea factory, labour and past production. A professional agricultural assessment will show you the real potential.

3. How land prices work (and the perch)

Land prices vary a lot. Two plots in the same district can have very different asking prices. The price usually depends on:

  • Price per perch and total land extent.
  • Distance from the nearest town.
  • Road frontage and access.
  • Electricity and water.
  • Shape and slope of the land.
  • Neighbourhood and nearby development.
  • Ownership and legal status.
  • Local demand.

Understanding the perch

In Sri Lanka land is advertised in perches, roods, acres and square feet.

  • 1 perch = 272.25 square feet
  • 1 rood = 40 perches
  • 1 acre = 160 perches
Example: a 20-perch plot advertised at LKR 8,000,000.
Price per perch = LKR 8,000,000 ÷ 20 = LKR 400,000.

4. Looking for low-budget land?

You do not have to start with expensive land. On a smaller budget, look at:

  • Land outside the main town centres.
  • Smaller residential plots.
  • Rural residential land.
  • Farmland that suits your purpose.
  • Developing areas that already have basic facilities.

A low price does not always mean a good buy. Find out why the land is cheaper. It may have poor road access, drainage problems, an unclear boundary, difficult terrain or building restrictions. Compare similar plots before you decide.

5. What to check before you pay

This is the most important stage. Before you pay an advance or sign anything, arrange independent checks.

  1. Deed and ownership. Ask for the ownership documents and have an independent Sri Lankan Attorney-at-Law or Notary Public check the title history and legal position.
  2. Survey plan. Compare the land extent, boundaries and measurements with the survey plan.
  3. Road access. Visit the land and confirm there is proper legal and practical access.
  4. Electricity and water. Confirm which utilities are available and what connecting them would involve.
  5. Restrictions. Ask the relevant authorities about zoning, planning, environmental and building restrictions.
  6. Price. Compare similar plots in the same area, and note how recent and reliable the price information is.
  7. Visit in person. Never decide from photos or social media posts alone.

6. The buying steps

The exact order can differ from case to case, and your lawyer will guide you. In general a purchase goes like this:

  1. Agree the basics. Agree the price, the land extent and the timeline with the seller. Be careful about paying any advance before the checks in step 2 are done.
  2. Legal and survey checks. Your lawyer checks the title history, and you compare the land with the survey plan.
  3. Agreement to sell. If the checks are clear, a written agreement records the price, the terms and the dates.
  4. Deed of transfer. The deed is prepared and signed before a Notary Public.
  5. Registration. The deed is registered at the Land Registry so that your ownership is recorded.
  6. Taxes and fees. Stamp duty and other charges apply to the transfer. Ask your lawyer which ones apply to your purchase and what they cost.
  7. Collect your registered deed and keep the originals safe.

Important for overseas buyers: Foreign ownership of land in Sri Lanka is restricted by law. Non-citizens generally cannot buy land outright, and long leases (up to 99 years) are the usual route. There are limited exceptions, and the rules have been amended more than once. See the Land (Restrictions on Alienation) Act No. 38 of 2014 and its amendments. If you are not a Sri Lankan citizen, or you hold dual citizenship or have changed citizenship, get specific advice from a Sri Lankan lawyer before you pay anything.
